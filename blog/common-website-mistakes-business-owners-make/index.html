10 Common Website Mistakes Business Owners Make

The avoidable website issues that reduce enquiries, trust and local visibility.

Published 2026-06-02Updated 2026-06-29
In this guideStart with the business outcomeWhat to checkCommon mistakesNext steps

Most underperforming websites fail for practical reasons: unclear messaging, slow pages, weak calls to action, outdated information and forms that are awkward to use on a phone. These problems quietly reduce enquiries even when the design looks acceptable.

A good business website should tell visitors who you help, what you do, where you work, why they can trust you and what to do next. If people have to hunt for that information, many will leave and contact a competitor instead.

Start with the business outcome

Review every page through the eyes of a busy customer. They are usually comparing options quickly, often from a mobile device, and need confidence before they call, book or request a quote.

The goal is not to add more decoration. The goal is to remove friction between the visitor's problem and the action you want them to take.

What small businesses should check

Message clarity

Your homepage should explain the service, location and next step within seconds. Avoid vague claims that could apply to any business.

Mobile experience

Buttons, forms, maps and menus must work comfortably on smaller screens. Many local searches happen on phones.

Trust signals

Add reviews, accreditations, case studies, photos, guarantees, clear contact details and realistic service information.

Common mistakes to avoid

Common mistakes include hiding contact details, using oversized images, leaving old prices online, burying service areas, publishing thin content and forgetting to test enquiry forms after updates.

Practical next steps

Ask three people unfamiliar with your business to find your phone number, understand your main service and submit a test enquiry. Their feedback will quickly show where the website is losing potential customers.
