How Much Does a Business Website Cost in the UK?

A practical guide to website pricing, scope and ongoing costs for UK small businesses.

Published 2026-06-01Updated 2026-06-29
In this guideStart with the business outcomeWhat to checkCommon mistakesNext steps

A business website quote should explain what is being built, how much content is included, what happens after launch and which costs repeat each year. A simple brochure site for a small local company is not priced the same as a lead-generation site with booking forms, integrations, copywriting, photography, analytics and ongoing support.

The biggest cost drivers are page count, design complexity, content preparation, custom functionality, hosting quality and maintenance. Ask whether the price includes discovery, mobile design, on-page SEO basics, contact form testing, security setup, backups, training and post-launch fixes.

Start with the business outcome

Decide whether the site needs to win trust, generate enquiries, sell products, reduce phone calls or support existing customers. Once the outcome is clear, you can separate essentials from nice-to-have extras and avoid paying for features that do not help the business.

For many UK small businesses, a sensible website budget is best viewed as a launch cost plus a monthly care cost. The monthly element should cover secure hosting, updates, backups, monitoring and help when something needs changing.

What small businesses should check

Scope

Confirm the number of pages, revision rounds, forms, integrations and content tasks included. If copywriting or image sourcing is not included, plan who will provide it and when.

Ongoing costs

Check domain renewal, hosting, SSL, email, plugin licences, maintenance and support response times. Cheap launch prices can become expensive if essential care is missing.

Ownership

Make sure you own the content, domain, accounts and finished website files where appropriate. You should not be locked out of the business asset you paid for.

Common mistakes to avoid

Avoid comparing quotes by headline price alone. One supplier may include planning, responsive design, technical setup and support while another only includes a template build. Also avoid launching without analytics, tested forms or a maintenance plan.

Practical next steps

Write a one-page brief listing your target customers, key services, required pages, must-have features and budget range. Mortify Software can then advise whether a lean brochure site, a conversion-focused build or a more advanced system is the right next step.
